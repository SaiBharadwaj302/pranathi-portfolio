# Blood Bank Rotation: Reflective Journal

**Cambridge Health Alliance (CHA), a Harvard Medical School affiliate, Cambridge, MA** | March 2026

**Instruments:** Immucor solid phase analyzer, manual tube methods

---

## Overview

My blood bank rotation at Cambridge Health Alliance (CHA) was one of the most engaging and rewarding experiences of my clinical training. During this rotation, I was exposed to a wide range of immunohematology procedures including blood typing and antibody screening, antibody identification (ABID), direct antiglobulin testing (DAT), crossmatching (both immediate spin and full AHG), antigen typing, fetal blood testing, cord blood workups, antibody titrations, and issuing blood units. I also gained experience entering and verifying units in the system, which helped me understand the importance of accuracy and traceability in blood bank operations.

## A Case That Stood Out

One of the most interesting cases I encountered was a delayed transfusion reaction workup involving anti-E. The patient had no prior history of alloantibodies but returned three months later with complaints of anemia. Upon further testing, anti-E was identified. It was fascinating to see how an antibody that was previously undetectable became clinically significant later. This case helped me connect theory with real life application and deepened my understanding of immune responses in transfusion medicine.

## Building Technical Skill

I particularly enjoyed the investigative nature of blood bank work. Every test and reaction made sense when connected back to the theory I had learned in class. Initially, performing tests using different methodologies felt overwhelming, but after the first few attempts, I became more confident and was able to understand the workflow better. Over time, I improved my technical skills, especially in interpreting weak reactions such as 1+ agglutination in solid phase testing, where even a slight reduction in button size or a faint red halo can indicate a positive reaction.

## How My Perspective Changed

Before starting this rotation, I assumed blood bank work was mostly independent and involved handling one task at a time. However, this experience completely changed my perspective. I learned that blood bank requires strong multitasking skills, attention to detail, and excellent organization. I often had to manage multiple samples at once while carefully documenting every step in my notebook to ensure nothing was missed. I also realized how critical proper documentation is, not just for lab records but for patient safety. Verifying patient identifiers such as wristband number, MRN, name, and date of birth before issuing blood products is essential. I also learned the importance of obtaining proper signatures and maintaining clear communication during blood issuance, especially during high pressure situations like Massive Transfusion Protocols (MTP).

## Mentorship

The staff at CHA were incredibly supportive and played a huge role in my learning experience. Although I initially had to cover extra days during my rotation, my supervisor consistently checked on my progress and created a positive learning environment. She took the time to discuss transfusion reactions, MTP protocols, and blood product usage with me. I was also given additional worksheets, including ABID panels and Kleihauer-Betke (KB) test exercises, which helped reinforce my understanding beyond bench work. Her willingness to answer all my questions made her an excellent mentor and teacher.

## Teamwork and Communication

While I did not have direct patient interaction, I frequently interacted with nurses and other healthcare staff who came to the blood bank to collect blood products. These interactions emphasized the importance of communication and teamwork. I learned how to explain proper handling of blood products, such as maintaining temperature in ice boxes, and how to verify documentation before issuing units. I also observed protocols for returning unused blood units to inventory, which highlighted the importance of maintaining product integrity and safety.

## Growing Interest in Blood Bank

This rotation significantly strengthened my interest in blood bank as a discipline. I truly enjoyed being able to apply what I learned in class to real clinical scenarios. The similarity between my academic training and actual lab practice made the transition smoother, and I appreciated the opportunity to perform many tests manually rather than relying solely on instrumentation. I also learned about the advantages and limitations of solid phase testing, which gave me a more well rounded perspective.

## Challenges

However, there were challenges as well. During high workloads, especially with multiple STAT samples or an influx of cord blood testing from labor and delivery, managing everything efficiently became difficult. These challenges were further intensified when instruments were down, requiring more manual testing. I also learned that second shift can be particularly busy due to incoming samples from external clinics. Despite these challenges, the experience taught me how to stay organized and work under pressure. Additionally, I had the opportunity to meet an alumnus from UMass Dartmouth, which was inspiring and motivating.

## Reflection

Overall, this rotation made me appreciate the critical role of a clinical laboratory scientist in ensuring patient safety and effective transfusion practices. Blood bank is a field that requires precision, critical thinking, and strong communication skills, and this experience has made me more confident in my abilities.

## Advice for Future Students

For future students, I would recommend going into the blood bank rotation with an open mind and a willingness to learn. Take advantage of your supervisors' knowledge, as they have valuable real world experience to share. Reviewing key concepts before starting the rotation can be very helpful. Most importantly, practice antibody identification panels as much as possible, as they are essential to success in this area. Staying organized and asking questions whenever unsure will make the experience much more rewarding.
