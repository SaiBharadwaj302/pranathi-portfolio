# Phlebotomy Rotation: Reflective Journal

**MIT Health, Cambridge, MA** | January 27 to February 2, 2026

---

## Week in Review

My first day at MIT Health, Tuesday, January 27, began shortly after a snowstorm, which made the morning feel both exciting and slightly intimidating. Upon arrival, I was introduced to the staff and given a brief tour of the phlebotomy department. To start, I was encouraged to feel patients' veins, which helped me learn to identify vein location, depth, and texture without the immediate pressure of performing a procedure. This step allowed me to gain tactile familiarity and build confidence in handling patient arms gently.

Once I felt more comfortable, I practiced venipuncture on simulation gloves marked with the median, cephalic, and basilic veins. This practice allowed me to refine my hand positioning, needle insertion angles, and tube handling in a controlled environment. After gaining confidence on the simulation, I began performing my first supervised venipunctures on real patients, starting with those who had well defined veins. I started with butterfly needles and later used straight needles. Although I was nervous initially, the guidance of Ms. Marie and the positive feedback from patients helped me gradually build confidence, while also highlighting the importance of combining technical skill with empathy and patient centered care.

On Wednesday, January 28, my second day, I continued drawing blood from patients, but I also began shadowing Ms. Toya, who provided invaluable guidance throughout the day. She explained subtle techniques for vein stabilization, needle insertion, and patient interaction. She even handed me her personal phlebotomy guide, which became a crucial resource for me. The guide helped me understand the order of draw, proper needle angles, tourniquet placement, risk factors, and common complications, giving me both theoretical and practical insight. With her supervision and the guide as reference, I was able to refine my technique, improve patient comfort, and begin drawing more challenging veins with confidence.

Thursday, January 29, was both challenging and educational. During a routine venipuncture, a patient unexpectedly fainted, requiring immediate intervention by the emergency department. This incident reminded me that phlebotomy, while routine in many ways, always requires vigilance, preparation, and adherence to safety protocols. Aside from this incident, I continued performing blood draws on other patients, applying the techniques I had learned from both Ms. Marie and Ms. Toya. This day emphasized the importance of flexibility, observation, and adjusting technique to accommodate each patient's unique needs, such as vein size, age, or anxiety level.

By Friday, January 30, I felt more confident and efficient in my procedure. My focus was on minimizing patient discomfort while maintaining accuracy and proper specimen collection. Ms. Toya's guidance, along with the phlebotomy guide she provided, allowed me to refine subtle aspects of my technique, such as proper needle angle, gentle insertion, and accurate tube handling. I also learned to pay attention to small cues from patients, adjusting my approach based on their comfort level. This day reinforced the understanding that phlebotomy is a careful balance of technical skill, patient empathy, and attention to detail.

Monday, February 2, was the final day of my rotation. By this point, I was performing multiple venipunctures with increased independence, and patients provided positive feedback regarding the smoothness and minimal discomfort of their draws. Reflecting on the week, I realized how much I had progressed, from nervousness on the first day to performing procedures confidently and competently by the last day. This rotation helped me understand that phlebotomy requires technical precision, patient centered care, and continuous learning. Every element, from vein selection and needle handling to tube collection and communication, plays a role in both specimen quality and patient experience.

## Patient Interactions

Throughout the rotation, I observed that every patient responded differently to blood draws. Some were anxious, particularly younger patients, while others, including elderly patients, were calm and cooperative. I quickly learned that effective communication and reassurance are crucial to success. One of my earliest patients encouraged me, which helped reduce my initial nervousness. Most patients were appreciative of my efforts, while a few were hesitant, emphasizing the need for patience and professionalism. The incident with the fainting patient highlighted that preparation and vigilance are always necessary. Overall, these interactions reinforced the importance of empathy, clear communication, and professionalism in building patient trust and ensuring successful blood collection.

## Professional Responsibilities

This rotation made me realize that phlebotomy is both a technical and professional responsibility. Mastery requires repeated practice and observation, along with a strong understanding of patient needs and safety. Ensuring patient comfort and safety is paramount, including minimizing pain, anxiety, and potential complications. Protecting oneself is equally important, particularly to avoid needle stick injuries or exposure to infectious materials. Attention to detail, including proper tube handling, correct specimen volume, and gentle technique, is crucial for accurate laboratory results. The phlebotomy guide provided by Ms. Toya was especially helpful in reinforcing these principles, particularly regarding the order of draw, insertion angles, tourniquet use, and risk management. Observation, reflection, and guidance from experienced staff were instrumental in my growth throughout the rotation.

## Personal Growth

Initially, I was nervous about performing venipunctures and concerned about making mistakes. By the end of the rotation, I had gained confidence and competence. I learned that phlebotomy is not just a technical skill but a profession that combines precision, adaptability, and patient care. Observing experienced staff, practicing on multiple patients, and applying lessons from Ms. Toya's guide helped me internalize best practices and refine my technique. I also developed a greater appreciation for the small details, such as needle angle, insertion depth, and tube handling, which affect both patient comfort and specimen integrity. Overall, this experience strengthened my passion for phlebotomy and my understanding of the responsibilities it entails.

The rotation was initially intimidating, but starting with vein palpation and simulation before progressing to real patients helped build confidence gradually. I learned that attention to detail, observation, and practice are crucial for success. Each patient's experience depends on subtle aspects of the procedure, such as proper needle insertion, vein stabilization, and gentle technique. Adapting to patient needs, maintaining empathy, and staying professional are equally important. The guidance of Ms. Marie, shadowing Ms. Toya, and her phlebotomy guide all contributed to my understanding of best practices and the importance of patient centered care.

## Advice for Future Students

Phlebotomy requires both technical skill and compassionate patient care. I would advise future students to begin without fear and understand that learning comes with practice. Observe experienced phlebotomists closely, ask questions, and pay attention to every nuance, from vein selection and insertion angle to tube handling and patient comfort. Prepare your materials and organize your tubes before each draw, and always follow proper protocols to ensure safety and accuracy. Most importantly, focus on the patient experience, ensuring that each procedure is performed with care, precision, and empathy. With consistent practice, patience, and observation, skill and confidence naturally grow, as I experienced throughout my rotation.
