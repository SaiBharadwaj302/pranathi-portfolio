# Clinical Chemistry Rotation: Reflective Journal

**MIT Health, Cambridge, MA** | January to February 2026

**Instruments:** Vitros 5600

---

## Description of Experience

My clinical rotation in the clinical chemistry department was an extremely valuable and intellectually stimulating experience that provided meaningful insight into the operational and analytical aspects of laboratory medicine. I had the opportunity to work alongside medical laboratory scientists, observe their workflow, and progressively engage in routine laboratory activities. The environment fostered both learning and critical inquiry, allowing me to expand my knowledge on a daily basis.

A significant portion of my rotation involved working with the Vitros 5600 analyzer. At the outset, the complexity of the instrument was somewhat intimidating; however, as I became more familiar with its components and workflow, I developed a strong appreciation for its functionality and integration. Observing the processing of patient samples, the execution of analytical methods, and the reporting of results within chemistry panels enhanced my understanding of automated laboratory systems and their role in delivering accurate and timely patient data.

Overall, this rotation strengthened my confidence, technical curiosity, and appreciation for the precision inherent in clinical chemistry.

## Change in Understanding of CLS Responsibilities

Prior to this rotation, I perceived the role of a clinical laboratory scientist (CLS) primarily as one focused on sample analysis and result reporting. This experience significantly expanded my understanding, highlighting the complexity of the role and the level of responsibility required to ensure analytical accuracy and clinical reliability.

I developed a deeper appreciation for the importance of understanding test methodologies and analytical processes. By inquiring about how specific assays are performed, I learned that certain tests require dilutions that may either be executed automatically by the instrument or require the manual loading of specific diluents.

Additionally, I gained insight into calculated parameters such as indirect bilirubin. This reinforced the concept that not all reported values are directly measured.

I also learned about reagent integrity, specimen stability, delta checks, and the impact of preanalytical variables such as delays in processing and improper centrifugation.

## Interactions with the Healthcare Team

Although direct patient interaction was limited, I observed strong collaboration with nurses, physicians, and phlebotomy staff. Communication regarding critical values and specimen issues emphasized the importance of teamwork in patient care.

## Impact on My Perception

This rotation reshaped my perception of clinical laboratory science as a highly analytical and responsibility driven field. I recognized the importance of critical thinking in validating results and ensuring clinical relevance.

## Advice for Future Students

Do not be intimidated by instruments. Ask questions, understand methodologies, and correlate lab values with clinical conditions to develop strong critical thinking skills.
