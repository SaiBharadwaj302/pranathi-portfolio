# Microbiology Rotation: Reflective Journal

**Cambridge Health Alliance (CHA), a Harvard Medical School affiliate, Cambridge, MA** | March 2026

**Instruments:** MALDI-TOF, MicroScan WalkAway, Panther, GeneXpert, BD MAX, BACTEC FX

---

## Overview

My microbiology rotation at CHA (Cambridge Health Alliance) was a highly enriching and formative experience that significantly advanced my development as a medical laboratory science student. This rotation provided valuable exposure to a high complexity clinical laboratory, allowing me to integrate foundational microbiological principles with advanced diagnostic technologies while strengthening both my technical proficiency and clinical reasoning skills.

## Bench Experience

During this rotation, I gained experience across multiple benches, including blood culture, specimen planting, PCR, and specialized areas such as throat and urine cultures. At the blood culture bench, I developed a strong appreciation for the critical role of early detection of bacteremia and sepsis. Monitoring culture positivity, performing Gram stains, and ensuring timely communication of preliminary results emphasized the direct impact of laboratory findings on patient outcomes.

At the specimen planting bench, I refined my aseptic technique and reinforced the importance of proper inoculation methods to ensure accurate organism isolation. Working with a variety of selective and differential media enhanced my ability to interpret colony morphology, hemolytic patterns, and lactose fermentation characteristics. In the throat and urine benches, I improved both my efficiency and accuracy in identifying common pathogens, including beta-hemolytic *Streptococcus*, while maintaining careful distinction between contamination and true infection.

## Advanced Diagnostic Platforms

A major highlight of my rotation was my exposure to advanced diagnostic platforms, including MALDI-TOF, MicroScan, PCR systems, and GeneXpert. Transitioning from primarily manual biochemical testing in an academic setting to automated and molecular diagnostics initially required adaptation. However, this experience ultimately strengthened my understanding of how these technologies enhance accuracy, reduce turnaround time, and support standardized results.

Utilizing MALDI-TOF for rapid organism identification and MicroScan for antimicrobial susceptibility testing demonstrated the integration of technology with classical microbiological principles. Additionally, working on the PCR bench, including GeneXpert systems, reinforced the importance of molecular diagnostics in the sensitive and specific detection of pathogens such as *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* and *Chlamydia trachomatis*.

## Organisms Encountered

Throughout the rotation, I encountered a diverse range of clinically significant organisms, including *Helicobacter pylori*, beta-hemolytic *Streptococcus*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Aeromonas* species, and the less commonly observed *Eikenella corrodens*. Exposure to parasitology, including *Schistosoma*, *Entamoeba coli*, and *Giardia duodenalis*, further broadened my understanding of infectious diseases across different body systems and patient populations.

## Key Learning

One of the most impactful learning experiences during this rotation was adapting to the use of automated systems, as my prior training emphasized manual biochemical testing. This transition challenged me to think beyond procedural steps and focus more on data interpretation and clinical correlation. Over time, I became more confident in navigating these systems while maintaining a strong foundation in traditional microbiological methods.

## Reflection

Overall, this rotation played a crucial role in shaping my professional growth. It enhanced my ability to work efficiently in a fast paced clinical environment while maintaining accuracy, attention to detail, and adherence to quality and safety standards. Most importantly, it reinforced the essential role of the microbiology laboratory in guiding diagnosis, treatment, and patient care.

My experience at CHA has strengthened both my technical competencies and critical thinking skills, while deepening my understanding of the clinical relevance of microbiology.
